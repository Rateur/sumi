---
title: Tuck
description: Put the island away in a line under the top edge, and bring it back.
---

The island can tuck into a thin line under the top edge of your main screen, out of the way of tabs and title bars. It comes back when something needs it.

## Tuck it

- **Rest the cursor on the island, then scroll up.** One wheel notch is enough. A scroll on a cursor that is only passing over does nothing.
- **In the tray icon's menu**, choose **Tuck the island**.
- **Drag a window.** While you move or resize a window, the island tucks so Snap layouts has the top of the screen, and comes back when you let go. If it was already tucked, it stays tucked.

## It tucks itself

When the island shows only the time or a running timer, it tucks itself after 10 seconds at rest: no cursor on it, nothing open. Music, a call, the microphone or camera dot, the command bar and a timer that ended keep it out. Settings, in 0.9, will let you choose for each one.

## Bring it back

- **Rest the cursor on the line**, scroll down on it, or pull it down.
- **Press the command bar's shortcut**: the bar opens from the line.
- **In the tray icon's menu**, choose **Bring the island back**.
- **Something new** brings it back: a call, music that starts, a timer that ends, an app using the microphone or the camera.

It comes back to what it would show then: the time, the music or the timer. After you bring it back by hand, the 10 seconds start over once the cursor leaves it.

While an app uses the microphone or the camera, the island stays out, with its dot.

## Limits

- The scroll reaches the island through Windows' **Scroll inactive windows when I hover over them** setting, on by default. With it off, use the tray icon.
- The line catches the cursor 12 px down from the top edge, in the middle of the screen: over the top of a maximized browser's middle tab, a cursor that rests there brings the island back.
